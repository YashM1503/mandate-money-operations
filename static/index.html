Decision boundary

Human authority. Deterministic release controls.

  1. An investigation explains the evidence. Model output cannot approve or execute a payment.
  2. A record copied from a bank-change request retains that origin. Multiple copies do not become independent verification.
  3. An analyst records a check through the established vendor contact. A separate controller approves the exact payment version.
  4. The API rechecks current evidence, cash constraints, authority, and approval when releasing a simulated ledger entry.
  5. Changed intent, expired approval, or revoked authority requires renewed review. Idempotency prevents duplicate ledger entries.
Demo scope

Clear boundaries are part of trust.

The retailer, invoices, vendor contacts, cash balances, and ledger are synthetic. No payment network or bank is connected. Contact verification is a human attestation in the demo, not proof that a real call occurred. Cash checks use the supplied snapshot and commitments; they do not forecast liquidity. The journal supports integrity checking, not a claim of immutable storage or regulatory certification.
